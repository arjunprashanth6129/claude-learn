# Mode: study (a deep dive)

The learner wants to master this — usually for their academics — and may have given you their own material (textbook chapter, notes, past papers). Run the teach skill's full process, probe → plan → teach, with these specifics:

1. **Probe.** Map the learner's edge with graded questions (header `Probe`), exactly as Phase 1 describes. Find out their goal with a question (e.g. header `Goal`): exam and board, depth, deadline.
2. **Scope.** If they gave material, ask `learn-agent reader` for an outline of it (sections, page numbers, key definitions, the types of exercises), and plan from it — their material defines the syllabus. Use the researcher only for gaps the material doesn't cover.
3. **Plan.** Group the nodes into **topics** (sections of the subject, e.g. "Preparation", "Physical properties", "Reactions"). Write the approach and the dependency map, topics shown as groups, as visible text, then ask for approval (header `Plan`). Wait for the go-ahead.
4. **Teach, topic by topic.** For each node in the topic: `## <node name>`, motivate → establish → connect in full, then a quiz-check (header `Quiz`). Fetch the relevant section from the reader when you reach it, so you teach what their material says.
5. **End of each topic.** Write `## Notes: <topic name>` — tight revision notes for the whole topic: definitions, formulas and reactions (in LaTeX), conditions and exceptions, and common exam mistakes. Then a topic quiz of 2–3 questions (header `Review`) that mix the topic's nodes. Re-teach anything missed before moving to the next topic.
6. **End of the session.** When all topics are done, a final quiz of 4–6 questions across topics (header `Final`), then `## Key takeaways` and a short list of what to revise next, based on what they missed.

If the session is getting long, it is fine to stop at the end of a topic: summarise where you are and what's next, so they can continue in a new session.
